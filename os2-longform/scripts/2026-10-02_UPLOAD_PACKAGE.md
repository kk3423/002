DESCRIPTION:
In January 2026, federal investigators said eight American drivers had been killed — not by their crashes, but by the airbag in front of them. By April, reporting on NHTSA's initial findings put the count at ten. The parts involved weren't installed at a factory. They were replacement airbag inflators that NHTSA says were likely illegally imported, linked to a Chinese company known as DTN. And that is only one of three airbag problems still on American roads.

This automotive safety documentary follows all three:

• Substandard replacement airbag inflators linked to DTN, which, according to NHTSA, were found in used cars with salvage or rebuilt titles. Many reports identify these parts by the marking "DTN60DB." We were not able to verify that marking against NHTSA's own documents, so in the video we refer to them as DTN inflators.
• ARC airbag inflators. In 2023, NHTSA demanded a recall of 67 million inflators, ARC Automotive refused, and the formal push grew to cover 52 million inflators made by ARC and Delphi.
• The Takata airbag recall: free, years old, and still not finished. "Do not drive" warnings went out as recently as February 2026, and NHTSA released new public service ads in September 2026.

You'll see what's actually inside an airbag inflator, why replacement airbags installed after a crash are so hard for the vehicle recall system to track, how NHTSA defines a safety defect, and what we could, and could not, verify. When a claim comes from a news report or a company statement, we say so.

If you own or are shopping for used cars, start with the checklist at 45:19. It covers how to check your VIN or license plate at NHTSA.gov, why your car's history matters if its airbags have ever deployed, and who should inspect an airbag. (Not you.)

This video is for information only and is not legal or mechanical advice. Never attempt to open, remove or inspect airbag components yourself.
