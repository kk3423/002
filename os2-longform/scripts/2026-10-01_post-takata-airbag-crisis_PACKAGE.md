### Checks performed
1. Every factual sentence carries a source tag that maps to a transcript line in `verified_transcripts.md`.
2. Quotes were checked against the transcripts. Light cleanup of auto-captions is marked with brackets: "[are]" in N2, "[and]" in N2, "[that]" in N1.
3. "Initial" is never converted to "final" (DTN April findings; ARC 2023 action).
4. "Likely illegally imported" is never shortened to "illegal" as the channel's own assertion. The anchor-line "illegally imported" in N2 is **not** used.
5. "Counterfeit" is **not used** as a description of the DTN inflators. It appears only once, inside the verbatim title of NHTSA's 2012 video ("NHTSA Airbag Counterfeit Final"). The agency's word for DTN, "substandard", is used. NBC's paraphrase "cheap, illegal part" was rewritten as "cheap substitute" to keep the "likely" discipline.
6. ARC and DTN positions are always attributed ("ARC said…", "the company says on its website…").
7. Joyson is not mentioned at all.
8. Lawsuit facts are labeled as allegations. The victim is not named. No injury detail.
9. Unresolved status (DTN count after April, ban status, ARC outcome) is stated as a limit of our verification, never as a negative fact.
10. Number conflicts are handled on screen:
    - 67M vs 52M: two different steps, reason not explained.
    - ARC deaths: conservative wording (C39b).
    - Takata unrepaired: 225k Stellantis vs ~5M Carfax, with different scopes stated.

### Residual risks
- **NTD (Tier 2) is the sole source** for the 6-death (Oct 2025) and 10-death (Apr 2026) counts, the "unknown importers" phrase, the Duffy quote and the 10,000 estimate.
  - Mitigation: each is attributed. NBC (Tier 1) independently confirms the 8-death January count and the "substandard" / likely-illegal framing.
  - **If anyone on the team can open NHTSA's April 2026 documents before publishing, confirm the four NTD-only items.**
- Auto-caption transcripts can mishear words. Numbers used were cross-checked across at least two outlets where possible. Single-source numbers are marked in the ledger.
- WFAA's viewer story (N15) is one case. It is presented as an example, not a statistic.
